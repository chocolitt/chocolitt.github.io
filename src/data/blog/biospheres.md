<p>Biosphere 2 was an attempt by Space Biosphere Ventures (and the <a href="http://ecotechnics.edu/">Institute for Ecotechnics</a>) to develop an entirely closed, self-sustaining environment.  In 1991, 8 people went in, intending to stay inside for two years.  With the exception of one major injury, requiring medical intervention in the outside world, they succeeded -- despite declining oxygen levels and interpersonal conflict so severe that by the end of their mission, the group was barely on speaking terms.  The second mission lasted only a few months before members from the first crew returned to sabotage the biosphere.  In 1995, the $200 million building was sold to Columbia University, and then later ownership was transferred to the University of Arizona, where it serves as a research location and tourist attraction (which I hope to visit in a few months).</p><p>The biospherians themselves were a strange mixture of scientists and experimental theater performers, led by the charismatic <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_P._Allen">John Allen</a> and funded by billionaire (and, based on the design above, aspiring supervillain) <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Bass">Ed Bass</a>.  I've just purchased Rebecca Reider's <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00944C7KO/ref=dp-kindle-redirect?_encoding=UTF8&amp;btkr=1">Dreaming the Biosphere</a>, which I'll probably review here soon.  So stay posted if you'd like to know more.</p><p>Biosphere 2 is not the only attempt at a man-made closed ecological system:</p><ul><li>BIOS-3 was a similar project in the Soviet Union, begun in 1973.  The longest mission inside was 180 days.  I haven't been able to find much information on it -- if anyone knows the story, please let me know.</li><li>The <a href="http://hi-seas.org/">HI-SEAS project</a> is an attempt to simulate <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v--IqqusnNQ">life on Mars</a>, in the habitat below.  The latest mission lasted a year and ended in August 2016, and was apparently <a href="http://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2016/08/29/491794937/mars-mission-crew-emerges-from-yearlong-simulation-in-hawaii">extremely boring</a>, with the exception of a breakdown in the water treatment system -- a disaster that could have killed everyone on the mission, or at least forced them to open the door.</li></ul>

<ul><li>There have been many attempts to create small closed ecological systems, including some by the original Biosphere 2 team.  Indeed, they created some while inside Biosphere 2 -- a biosphere in a biosphere in a biosphere.  These are now a pretty common <a href="http://www2.nau.edu/lrm22/lessons/bottle_biology/">elementary school activity</a>, and some <a href="https://www.amazon.com/EcoSphere-Closed-Aquatic-Ecosystem-Sphere/dp/B005IZOB5M/ref=sr_1_1?s=pet-supplies&amp;ie=UTF8&amp;qid=1476841529&amp;sr=1-1&amp;keywords=biosphere">beautiful examples</a> can be purchased on Amazon. <ul><li>Biospheres are a staple in fiction.  The widely panned <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4EWikCCfHJw">Bio-Dome</a>, in which two idiots are trapped in a habitat clearly modeled after Biosphere 2, is an early example.  <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ej3ioOneTy8">The Martian</a> is a recent (and excellent) entry in the genre.</li></ul><p>If anyone knows of other examples of closed ecological systems, especially ones in which people have lived (aside from e.g. spacecraft), I'd be thrilled to hear about them.</p>
