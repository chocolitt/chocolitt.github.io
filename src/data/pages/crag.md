papers</h2><p>A preliminary list of papers can be found below, but feel free to suggest any paper you’re interested in, or to ask me for other suggestions. Please claim a paper and date — as you read the paper, you should come talk to me about how to best present it.</p><ul><li><p>Araujo-Kollár, <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0203173.pdf">Rational curves on varieties</a> — studies rational curves on algebraic varieties, building on “bend-and-break”</p></li><li><p>Atiyah, <a href="https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/plms/s3-7.1.414">Vector bundles over an elliptic curve</a> — a classification of vector bundles on an elliptic curve. A beautiful, fairly elementary paper, and a good prequel to the notion of stability for vector bundles.</p></li><li><p>Beauville-Voisin, <a href="https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~claire.voisin/Articlesweb/Chowams.pdf">On the Chow ring of a K3 surface</a> — constructs a very interesting 0-cycle on K3 surfaces</p></li><li><p>Behrend-Fantechi, <a href="https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s002220050136.pdf">The intrinsic normal cone</a> — Explains how to construct <em>virtual fundamental classes</em> in many settings</p></li><li><p>Buhler-Reichstein, <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1000144403695">On the essential dimension of a finite group</a> — Introduces the notion of “essential dimension” and computes it in some cases.</p></li><li><p>Borel-Serre, <a href="http://www.numdam.org/article/BSMF_1958__86__97_0.pdf">Le théorème de Riemann–Roch, d'après A. Grothendieck</a> — a proof of the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem. We should possibly follow this talk with one about an application of GRR. See translation <a href="https://thosgood.com/translations/">here</a>, by Tim Hosgood.</p></li><li><p>Chan-Galatius-Payne, <a href="https://www.ams.org/journals/jams/2021-34-02/S0894-0347-2021-00965-7/">Tropical curves, graph complexes, and top weight cohomology of \(M_g\)</a> — uses tropical techniques to show that \(M_g\) has lots of non-tautological cohomology</p></li><li><p>Clemens-Griffiths, <a href="https://www.math.ucsd.edu/~eizadi/207A-2016/ClemensGriffiths.pdf">The intermediate Jacobian of a cubic threefold</a> — shows that smooth cubic threefolds aren’t rational.</p></li><li><p>Ceresa, <a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/2007078?seq=1#metadata_info_tab_contents">\(C\) is not algebraically equivalent to \(-C\) in its Jacobian</a> — shows that for a generic curve of genus at least 3, the image of the curve under the Abel-Jacobi  map is not algebraically equivalent to its inverse. This is interesting because these two cycles are homologically equivalent.</p></li><li><p>Deligne, <a href="https://publications.ias.edu/node/402">Un théorème de finitude pour la monodromie</a> — a finiteness result about representations of fundamental groups which underly (polarizable) variations of Hodge structure. This is the “function field” version of the Shafarevich conjecture.</p></li><li><p>Deligne-Illusie, <a href="https://publications.ias.edu/node/403">Relèvements modulo \(p^2\) et décomposition du complexe de <em>de Rham</em></a><em> — </em>a proof that the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence degenerates in characteristic zero, via characteristic p methods; also, a purely algebraic proof of the Kodaira vanishing theorem.</p></li><li><p>Deligne-Mumford, <a href="http://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_1969__36__75_0.pdf">The irreducibility of the space of curves of a given genus</a> — proves that the moduli space of curves \(M_g\) is irreducible</p></li><li><p>Deninger-Werner, <a href="http://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2005_4_38_4_553_0.pdf">Vector bundles on \(p\)-adic curves and parallel transport</a> — the beginning of non-abelian \(p\)-adic Hodge theory</p></li><li><p>Esnault-Kerz, <a href="http://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/esnault/preprints/helene/109-final.pdf">A finiteness theorem for Galois representations of function fields over finite fields (after Deligne)</a> — an l-adic variant of “<em>Un théorème de finitude pour la monodromie</em>.” A talk on this should focus on the case of curves (which is only sketched in the paper — come talk to me if you’re interested) and then spend a bit of time on how to deduce the case of higher-dimensional varieties.</p></li><li><p>Esnault-Mehta, <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00222-010-0250-2">Simply connected projective manifolds in characteristic \(p&gt;0\) have no nontrivial stratified bundles</a> — proves that two natural notions of “fundamental group” are closely related</p></li><li><p>Fontaine, <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01388584">Il n'y a pas de variété abélienne sur \(\mathbb{Z}\)</a> — proves what it says in the title, namely that there are no Abelian schemes over \(\text{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})\). </p></li><li><p>Fontaine, <a href="http://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1994__223__59_0/">Exposé II: Les corps des périodes \(p\)-adiques</a> — develops the period rings arising in \(p\)-adic Hodge theory</p></li><li><p>Grothendieck, <a href="https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~leila.schneps/grothendieckcircle/HodgeConj.pdf">Hodge's general conjecture is false for trivial reasons</a> — reformulates the generalized Hodge conjecture to avoid certain clever but “trivial” counterexamples.</p></li><li><p>Grothendieck, <a href="https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~leila.schneps/grothendieckcircle/Fourothers.pdf">Le groupe de Brauer I-III</a> — develops the theory of the Brauer group of a scheme. Incredibly fun and incredibly useful.</p></li><li><p>Grothendieck, <a href="http://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_1966__29__95_0.pdf">On the de Rham cohomology of algebraic varieties</a> — compares algebraic de Rham cohomology to the usual notion for manifolds, using resolution of singularities.</p></li><li><p>Hassett-Pirutka-Tschinkel, <a href="https://cims.nyu.edu/~pirutka/quadrics7.pdf">Stable rationality of quadric surface bundles over surfaces</a> — shows that non-rationality does not specialize</p></li><li><p>Harer-Zagier, <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2FBF01390325">The Euler characteristic of the moduli space of curves</a> — computes the Euler characteristic of \(M_g\)</p></li><li><p>Honda, <a href="http://web.math.rochester.edu/people/faculty/doug/otherpapers/Honda-Ab.pdf">Isogeny classes of abelian varieties over finite fields</a> — classifies Abelian varieties over finite fields, up to isogeny, in terms of \(q\)-Weil numbers.</p></li><li><p>Katz, <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01389714">Algebraic solutions of differential equations (<em>p</em>-curvature and the Hodge filtration)</a> — formulates the \(p\)-curvature conjecture and proves it in the case of the Gauss-Manin connection.</p></li><li><p>Katz, <a href="http://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_1970__39__175_0.pdf">Nilpotent connections and the monodromy theorem: Applications of a result of Turrittin</a> — studies the local monodromy of the Gauss-Manin connection via mixed-characteristic methods. A great place to learn about algebraic differential equations.</p></li><li><p>Kontsevich-Tschinkel, <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00222-019-00870-9">Specialization of birational types</a> — shows that rationality specializes (for families of smooth varieties), via a simple and beautiful argument (motivated by the notion of “motivic nearby cycles,” which is much more elementary than it sounds).</p></li><li><p>Lubin-Tate, <a href="https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/4e3e/057a9c2e811e07b0cd20a54d38b5f746b9cb.pdf">Formal moduli for one-parameter formal groups</a> — constructs special formal groups which realize “explicit class field theory” for local fields. A talk on this should give context from class field theory, e.g. from Cassels-Frohlich.</p></li><li><p>Mori, <a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/1971241">Projective manifolds with ample tangent bundles</a> — proves Hartshorne’s conjecture, characterizing projective space as the only smooth projective variety with ample tangent bundle. Introduces “bend-and-break.”</p></li><li><p>Mumford, <a href="http://www.mathcs.emory.edu/~brussel/Scans/mumfordpicard.pdf">Picard groups of moduli problems</a> — computes the Picard group of \(\mathscr{M}_{1,1}\), without exactly saying that this is what it’s doing. A great prequel to the notion of a stack.</p></li><li><p>Narasimhan-Seshadri, <a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/1970710?seq=1">Stable and unitary vector bundles on a compact Riemann surface</a> — the beginning of non-abelian Hodge theory</p></li><li><p>Tate, Fourier analysis in number fields, and Hecke's zeta-functions (Tate’s thesis) — Introduces the adelic point of view on \(L\)-functions, and proves functional equations/analytic continuation in many cases. </p></li><li><p>Tate, <a href="https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-87942-5_12">p-divisible groups</a> — the beginning of the theory of \(p\)-divisible groups. A good prequel to \(p\)-adic Hodge theory.</p></li><li><p>Serre-Tate, <a href="https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Serre-Tate-Good_Reduction_of_Abelian_Varieties.pdf">Good reduction of abelian varieties</a> — proves the Nèron-Ogg-Shafarevich criterion for good reduction of abelian varieties, using Nèron models.</p></li><li><p>Vakil, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411469">Murphy’s law in algebraic geometry</a> — shows that moduli spaces of nice objects can be arbitrarily singular.</p></li><li><p>Voisin, <a href="https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~claire.voisin/Articlesweb/syzod.pdf">Green’s canonical syzygy conjecture for generic curves of odd genus</a> — Proves Green’s conjecture in many cases</p></li><li><p>Voisin, <a href="https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00222-003-0352-1.pdf">On the homotopy types of compact Kähler and complex projective manifolds</a> — provides the first examples of compact Kähler manifolds which are not homotopy equivalent to any complex projective variety.</p></li><li><p>Weil, <a href="https://www.projecteuclid.org/euclid.bams/1183513798">Numbers of solutions of equations in finite fields</a> — states the Weil conjectures and proves them for certain special hypersurfaces.</p></li></ul>
