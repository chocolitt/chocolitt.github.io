<p>This is the course page for the Fall 2022 Hodge theory course. We will loosely follow Voisin’s books, but with lots of supplemental material to be posted here.</p><p>Voisin, <a href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/hodge-theory-and-complex-algebraic-geometry-i/A6E52939BA107FFCB5A901D5B5D88025"><em>Hodge Theory and Complex Algebraic Geometry I</em></a></p><p>Voisin, <a href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/hodge-theory-and-complex-algebraic-geometry-ii/47F0D9D694725A426F9CAFDFA830DB11"><em>Hodge Theory and Complex Algebraic Geometry II</em></a></p><p>Office Hours: T1-2, BA6172</p><h2>Tentative Schedule</h2><p>Week 1: What is Hodge theory? Intro to complex manifolds, Kähler structures, etc.</p><p>Week 2: de Rham cohomology, Laplacians, elliptic operators</p><p>Week 3: The Hodge and Lefschetz decompositions, and the Hodge index theorem</p><p>Week 4: Hodge structures, polarizations, the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence</p><p>Week 5: Algebraic de Rham cohomology, comparison, Deligne-Illusie and reduction mod p</p><p>Week 6: Some applications — generic vanishing</p><p>Week 7: Variations of Hodge structure, period maps</p><p>Week 8: Some applications — IVHS, generic Torelli theorems, monodromy</p><p>Week 9: Cycle classes</p><h2>assessment</h2><p>Students will be asked to write a short note and give a short presentation on a topic of Hodge theory, individually or in pairs. Some possible topics include:</p><ul><li><p>The \(T^1\)-lifting theorem and the Tian-Todorov theorem (unobstructedness of Calabi-Yau manifolds) (lots of sources, e.g. <a href="/assets/squarespace/660f280f171a-tian-todorov.pdf">here</a>)</p></li><li><p><a href="https://projecteuclid.org/journals/kyoto-journal-of-mathematics/volume-8/issue-2/On-the-differentiation-of-De-Rham-cohomology-classes-with-respect/10.1215/kjm/1250524135.full">Katz-Oda’s</a> algebraic description of the Gauss-Manin connection</p></li><li><p>The <a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/2007078#metadata_info_tab_contents">Ceresa cycle is not always algebraically equivalent to zero</a> (there are now many proofs of this — we could even do more than one)</p></li><li><p><a href="https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~eizadi/207A-2016/ClemensGriffiths.pdf">The intermediate Jacobian of a cubic threefold</a></p></li><li><p><a href="http://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1983__50_2-3_325_0.pdf">Generic Torelli for hypersurfaces</a></p></li><li><p><a href="http://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1992__82_1_1_0/">André’s theorem of the fixed part</a></p></li><li><p>Bloch’s conjecture (Voisin II, Chapter 11 and Mumford’s <a href="https://projecteuclid.org/journals/kyoto-journal-of-mathematics/volume-9/issue-2/Rational-equivalence-of-0-cycles-on-surfaces/10.1215/kjm/1250523940.full"><em>Rational equivalence of zero-cycles on surfaces</em></a>) </p></li><li><p><a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01389853">Formality of compact Kähler manifolds</a></p></li></ul>
